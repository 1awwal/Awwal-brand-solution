How this course works

  1. Start at your level. Beginner, Intermediate and Professional each have their own curriculum. New to the subject? Begin at level one.
  2. Watch, then read the notes. Every session has one trusted YouTube tutorial and short session notes with the points to remember.
  3. Do the practice task. Skills come from doing. Every session ends with something to make.
  4. Earn a certificate per level. Mark sessions complete — progress is saved in this browser — and unlock a certificate at each level.

What you need

  • A camera: any camera with manual controls, or a recent smartphone. The beginner level works with a phone.
  • For flash lessons: one speedlight or small LED light. A window and a white card are enough to begin.
  • Somewhere to practise: friends, products from your kitchen, and your own street.
  • Time: about 3 hours of video across all three levels, plus practice.

Session 1.1

Photography basics

The core ideas every photographer needs before anything else.

Video referencePHOTOGRAPHY BASICS in 10 MINUTESDavid Manning · 11:10 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A photograph is made from light, a subject and a moment.
  • Learn your camera's manual or aperture-priority mode early.
  • Shoot often and review your photos: practice teaches more than gear.
Practice

Take 30 photos of one ordinary object and choose the three strongest.

Session 1.2

Exposure: aperture, shutter speed and ISO

How the three exposure settings work together and what each one changes in the picture.

Video referenceIntuitively Understand Exposure in 12 Minutes (With Real Examples)Never fStop · 12:46 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Aperture controls depth of field: a low f-number blurs the background.
  • Shutter speed controls motion: fast freezes, slow blurs.
  • ISO brightens the image but adds noise — raise it last.
Practice

Photograph the same scene three times, changing only one setting each time, and compare the results.

Session 1.3

Composition

How to arrange a frame so the viewer looks where you want.

Video reference8 IMPORTANT Composition Tips for Better PhotosJamie Windsor · 11:52 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Decide what the subject is, then remove everything that distracts from it.
  • Use the rule of thirds, leading lines and framing as starting points, not laws.
  • Move your feet: a step left or lower often fixes the picture.
Practice

Shoot one subject using five different compositions.

Session 1.4

Lenses and focal length

What focal length does to a picture and which lens suits which job.

Video referenceULTIMATE Beginner's Guide To Camera LensesTech Through The Lens · 8:36 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Wide lenses (16–35 mm) show the scene; long lenses (85 mm+) isolate the subject.
  • Longer focal lengths compress distance and flatter faces.
  • A 50 mm prime is an affordable lens that teaches you to move.
Practice

Photograph a person at your widest and longest focal lengths, keeping them the same size in the frame.

Session 1.5

Smartphone photography

How to get strong photos from the camera you always carry.

Video reference13 Smartphone Photography tips & tricksJamie Windsor · 18:26 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Clean the lens and tap to set focus and exposure.
  • Use the main lens whenever possible; avoid digital zoom.
  • Good light matters more than megapixels.
Practice

Create a set of five phone photos of your neighbourhood that work together as a series.

Session 2.1

Natural light portraits

How to find and shape flattering natural light.

Video reference10 Tips to IMPROVE your Natural Light PortraitsJulia Trotti · 10:19 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Soft light comes from large sources: open shade, a window, an overcast sky.
  • Turn the subject towards the light to brighten the eyes.
  • Shoot in the hour after sunrise or before sunset when you can.
Practice

Photograph one person in three kinds of light: window, open shade and backlight.

Session 2.2

Posing people who are not models

Simple directions that help anyone look relaxed in front of a camera.

Video reference5 EASY Tips To Pose People Who Aren't ModelsMatthew Ruderman · 7:56 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Give people something to do — walk, lean, look away — instead of asking them to pose.
  • Shift weight to the back foot and create space between arms and body.
  • Keep talking; silence makes people stiff.
Practice

Photograph a friend for ten minutes using only action prompts, never the word 'pose'.

Session 2.3

Flash basics

How flash works and how to stop it looking harsh.

Video referenceFlash photography for beginners part 1Mike Smith · 15:25 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Bounce the flash off a wall or ceiling for softer light.
  • Shutter speed controls the background; aperture and flash power control the subject.
  • Start in TTL, then learn manual power for consistency.
Practice

Take the same indoor portrait with direct flash and bounced flash and compare.

Session 2.4

Product photography at home

How to build a simple, repeatable product setup with almost no budget.

Video referenceDIY Product Photography – EASY, PROFESSIONAL LOOKING and CHEAP (w/ Camera or Phone)Will Simpson - Exploring Photography · 11:33 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A window, a white card and a clean background are enough.
  • Use a tripod so every product in a range is framed identically.
  • Clean the product carefully — the camera sees every fingerprint.
Practice

Photograph three products on a clean background with consistent lighting and framing.

Session 2.5

Food photography

How to light, style and frame food so it looks fresh and appetising.

Video reference10 FOOD Photography TIPS (From beginner to advanced) | Behind the sceneNicolas Doretti · 11:42 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Side or back light shows texture; front light flattens food.
  • Choose the angle for the dish: overhead for flat dishes, 45° for plates, straight on for stacks.
  • Style simply and shoot quickly while the food is fresh.
Practice

Photograph one dish from the three classic angles and choose the best.

Session 3.1

Studio lighting fundamentals

How to build portrait lighting with one, two and three lights.

Video referenceStudio Lighting Fundamentals for Extraordinary Portraits | Master Your CraftAdorama · 18:10 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Start with one light and add others only for a reason.
  • Key light shapes the face, fill controls shadow depth, rim separates from the background.
  • Bigger and closer light sources are softer.
Practice

Create three different portraits of the same person using a single light in three positions.

Session 3.2

Off-camera flash techniques

How to take the flash off the camera for shape and depth on location.

Video reference5 Off-Camera Flash Techniques Every New Photographer Needs to KnowMagMod · 15:20 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Light from about 45° to the side and slightly above for natural shape.
  • Set the background exposure first, then add flash for the subject.
  • A simple umbrella or softbox makes a small flash look expensive.
Practice

Shoot an outdoor portrait at sunset, balancing the sky with off-camera flash.

Session 3.3

Event photography

How to cover an event so the client gets every shot they need.

Video referenceEvent Photography Tips and Tricks (6 Shot Types)Mik Milman · 9:32 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Get a shot list from the client and arrive early.
  • Cover wide, medium, detail, people and key moments.
  • Carry spare batteries and cards — there are no retakes.
Practice

Cover a real gathering — a family event, a market, a workshop — and deliver 25 edited photos.

Session 3.4

Building a portfolio

How to choose and present work that wins the jobs you want.

Video referenceHow to Build a Professional Photography Portfolio in Under 10 MinutesEvan Ranft · 14:41 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Show only your best 15–20 images.
  • Show the kind of work you want to be hired for.
  • Group images into series so the portfolio tells a story.
Practice

Select and sequence 15 images for a portfolio in one niche.

Session 3.5

Getting clients and pricing

How photographers find clients and set prices that make a living.

Video referenceThe Photography Business Secret NOBODY Teaches YouThe Carty Method · 10:30 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Solve a business problem — more bookings, better product pages — not just 'nice photos'.
  • Calculate your cost of doing business before you set a price.
  • Agree usage, number of images and delivery date in writing.
Practice

Create a one-page price list with three packages and clear deliverables.

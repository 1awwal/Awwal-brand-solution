How this course works

  1. Start at your level. Beginner, Intermediate and Professional each have their own curriculum. New to the subject? Begin at level one.
  2. Watch, then read the notes. Every session has one trusted YouTube tutorial and short session notes with the points to remember.
  3. Do the practice task. Skills come from doing. Every session ends with something to make.
  4. Earn a certificate per level. Mark sessions complete — progress is saved in this browser — and unlock a certificate at each level.

What you need

  • A camera: a mirrorless camera or a recent smartphone. The beginner level works with a phone.
  • Sound: a simple lavalier or on-camera microphone makes the biggest difference.
  • Support: a tripod. A gimbal and one LED light are useful for later lessons.
  • Time: about 3 hours of video across all three levels, plus practice.

Session 1.1

Videography basics

The essential settings and ideas behind good-looking video.

Video reference80% Of Videography Basics In Less Than 10 minutesPat Kay · 8:40 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Choose resolution and frame rate before you shoot — they are hard to change later.
  • Lock exposure and white balance so they do not shift during a shot.
  • Stable, well-lit and clearly heard beats expensive gear.
Practice

Film a 30-second scene with locked exposure, white balance and focus.

Session 1.2

Frame rate and shutter speed

How frame rate and shutter speed create natural-looking motion.

Video referenceThe 180 Degree Rule of Shutter Speed with Video ExamplesPolarPro · 5:59 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Set shutter speed to about double the frame rate: 1/50 at 25 fps.
  • Shoot 50 or 60 fps only when you plan slow motion.
  • Use an ND filter outdoors to keep the shutter slow in bright light.
Practice

Film the same moving subject at 1/50 and 1/500 and compare the motion.

Session 1.3

Shot sizes

The standard shot sizes and what each one tells the viewer.

Video referenceUltimate Guide to Camera Shots: Every Shot Size Explained [The Shot List, Ep 1]StudioBinder · 12:44 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Wide shots show where we are; close-ups show how someone feels.
  • Change shot size between cuts so edits feel smooth.
  • Always film a wide, a medium and a close of every scene.
Practice

Film someone making tea or coffee using at least six different shot sizes.

Session 1.4

Camera movement

The main camera movements and when movement helps the story.

Video referenceUltimate Guide to Camera Movement — Every Camera Movement Technique Explained [The Shot List Ep6]StudioBinder · 29:09 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Pan and tilt turn the camera; track, push and pull move it.
  • Move for a reason: to reveal, to follow or to build feeling.
  • A steady static shot is better than a shaky move.
Practice

Film one subject three ways: static, a slow push-in and a tracking shot.

Session 1.5

Smartphone filmmaking

How to shoot professional-looking video on a phone.

Video referenceMaster Mobile Filmmaking in 20 MinutesTomorrows Filmmakers · 26:43 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Film in 4K at 25 or 30 fps and lock exposure and focus.
  • Use the rear main lens and hold the phone with two hands.
  • Add an external microphone before any other accessory.
Practice

Shoot a 30-second film about a place using only your phone.

Session 2.1

Three-point lighting

The classic lighting setup and how to adapt it to any room.

Video referenceFilmmaking 101 - Three Point Lighting TutorialDiCasaFilm · 10:15 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Key light is the main source; fill softens shadows; back light separates the subject.
  • Place the key about 45° to the side and slightly above eye level.
  • A window can be your key light and a white wall your fill.
Practice

Light a seated person with a three-point setup using whatever lights you have.

Session 2.2

Recording clean audio

How to capture clear sound, which matters more than picture quality.

Video referenceWhat I Wish I Knew as a Beginner Filmmaker | Basics of Audio for VideoSpencer Fackrell · 10:07 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Get the microphone as close to the speaker as possible.
  • Monitor with headphones and keep peaks around -12 dB.
  • Record 30 seconds of room tone for the edit.
Practice

Record the same sentence with your camera's microphone and with an external mic placed close. Compare.

Session 2.3

Filming an interview

How to set up, light and frame an interview in an ordinary room.

Video referenceHow to shoot a SIMPLE INTERVIEW in a Boring RoomEpic Light Media · 10:44 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Move the subject away from the wall to create depth.
  • Have them look just off-camera, towards the interviewer.
  • Frame on a third with space in the direction they are looking.
Practice

Film a two-minute interview with good light, clean sound and a tidy background.

Session 2.4

Shooting b-roll

How to shoot b-roll that gives the editor options and tells the story.

Video referenceB-ROLL (Beginner VS Pro)Epic Light Media · 9:11 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Shoot sequences: wide, medium, close and a detail of each action.
  • Hold every shot for at least ten seconds.
  • Vary angle and height, not just distance.
Practice

Shoot 20 b-roll shots of a person at work, including at least five close details.

Session 2.5

Gimbal technique

How to get smooth, deliberate movement from a gimbal.

Video referenceThese 4 GIMBAL TECHNIQUES Makes All The Difference!Peter Makholm · 6:05 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Balance the gimbal properly before switching it on.
  • Bend your knees and walk heel to toe.
  • Plan the move's start and end frame before recording.
Practice

Shoot five gimbal moves: push-in, pull-out, orbit, follow and reveal.

Session 3.1

Visual storytelling

How to tell a story with pictures rather than explanation.

Video referenceVisual Storytelling 101Film Riot · 13:37 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Every story needs a character, a goal and an obstacle.
  • Show, do not tell: let actions and images carry meaning.
  • Decide the feeling of each scene before choosing shots.
Practice

Tell a complete story in five shots with no dialogue.

Session 3.2

Shot lists and pre-production

How to plan a shoot on paper so the day runs smoothly.

Video referenceHow to Make a Shot List for Film: A Step-by-Step GuideStudioBinder · 12:05 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A shot list names every shot: size, angle, movement and subject.
  • Group shots by location and setup to save time.
  • Planning is cheaper than reshooting.
Practice

Write a shot list of 15–20 shots for a one-minute brand video.

Session 3.3

Exposing log footage

How to expose log profiles correctly for the best image in the grade.

Video referenceHow To Properly Expose Log FootageCarsten Schlag · 9:05 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Log keeps more highlight and shadow detail but needs grading.
  • Follow your camera maker's exposure guidance for its log profile.
  • Use zebras, false colour or the waveform rather than judging by eye.
Practice

Shoot the same scene in a standard profile and in log, then grade the log clip to match.

Skip this lesson if your camera or phone has no log profile.

Session 3.4

Shooting a product commercial

How to plan and shoot a short product advert.

Video referenceHow to Shoot a Commercial on Your Phone (Step-by-Step Guide)Learn Online Video · 14:15 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Start from one message: what should the viewer remember?
  • Mix hero shots, details and the product in use.
  • Control reflections and keep the product spotless.
Practice

Shoot a 20–30 second product advert with at least eight different shots.

Session 3.5

Getting clients

How to find your first paying video clients.

Video referenceHow to Get Clients Starting from 0 (Guide for Beginner Videographers)Jono and Jason · 12:48 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Start with local businesses whose problem you can see.
  • A short sample film opens more doors than a price list.
  • Quote per project and put scope, revisions and delivery in writing.
Practice

Make a list of ten local businesses and write a tailored video idea for three of them.

How this course works

  1. Start at your level. Beginner, Intermediate and Professional each have their own curriculum. New to the subject? Begin at level one.
  2. Watch, then read the notes. Every session has one trusted YouTube tutorial, session notes with the points to remember, and a part-by-part guide that opens the video at each timestamp.
  3. Do the practice task. Skills come from doing. Every session ends with something to make.
  4. Pass the module quiz. Each module ends with a short quiz. Pass it to complete the module — retry as often as you need.
  5. Earn a certificate per level. Mark sessions complete — progress is saved in this browser — and unlock a certificate at each level.

What you need

  • CapCut: the free desktop or mobile app. Some features shown in the videos need CapCut Pro, and availability varies by country — the notes point to the technique, not the paid button.
  • A phone or computer: every beginner session works on a phone. A computer is more comfortable from the intermediate level.
  • Footage: clips from your own phone. Shoot something simple — a coffee being made, a walk, a product on a table.
  • Time: about 4 hours of video across all three levels, plus practice.

CapCut on desktop

  6. 7:05
    Keyframes, speed and animation

  7. 8:54
    Colour, text, audio and export

    Correct colour before adding a filter, add text and music, then export at the resolution and frame rate of your footage.

CapCut on mobile

Editing a complete video on your phone.

Session notes
  • The mobile app has the same core tools in a toolbar at the bottom.

  6. Part 6
    Export

    Choose 1080p and the frame rate you filmed in for most social video. Watch the exported file once before posting.

Session 1.3

Cutting on the timeline

The three actions you will use more than any others: split, trim and delete.

Video referenceCapCut PC Tutorial: How To Cut, Trim And Split ClipsH2X Tech · 4:19 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Split cuts a clip in two at the playhead; trim drags the edge of a clip shorter or longer.
  • Cut out pauses, repeated words and anything that does not move the video forward.
  • Learn the split shortcut on day one — it is the fastest way to speed up.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. Part 1
    The playhead

    The vertical line on the timeline is the playhead. Every cut happens where it sits, so learn to move it precisely with the arrow keys and by zooming in.

  2. Part 2
    Split

    Split cuts the selected clip in two at the playhead. Make two splits around a mistake, select the middle piece and delete it.

  3. Part 3
    Trim

    Drag the start or end of a clip inward to shorten it. Trimming is faster than splitting when you only need to remove the beginning or the end.

  4. Part 4
    Delete and close gaps

    Deleting a section on the main track closes the gap automatically. On other tracks you may need to move clips back together.

  5. Part 5
    Shortcuts

    Learn the split shortcut and the delete-left and delete-right commands. Editors who use the keyboard cut several times faster.

  6. Part 6
    Cut on purpose

    Cut on a pause, a breath or a change of idea. Remove hesitation and repetition; keep natural rhythm so speech does not sound robotic.

Session 2.1

Text and title animation

Adding text that is readable and moves well.

Video referenceHow to Master Smooth Text Animations in CapCutZeven 7 · 16:12 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Use one or two fonts and keep text inside the safe area, away from platform buttons.
  • Make sure text contrasts with the background — add a stroke, shadow or backing if needed.
  • Keep animations short and consistent; the text should be on screen long enough to read twice.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 1:42
    Text basics

    Add a text layer, choose a font and set size, colour and spacing in the inspector. Decide the hierarchy: one main line, one supporting line.

  2. 3:58
    Bounce animation

    A small overshoot — the text grows slightly past its size and settles — makes an entrance feel lively. Keep it short, around a quarter of a second.

  3. 5:20
    Slide animations

    Animate position with keyframes so text slides in from below, and add easing so it slows into place rather than stopping abruptly.

  4. 8:42
    Text composition

    Combine sizes and weights to emphasise the key word. Align text to a clear edge and leave space around it.

  5. 10:44
    Clean product-style animation

    Simple, precise motion with consistent timing looks more professional than heavy effects. Reuse the same animation for every title in a video.

  6. 13:28
    Highlighting words

    A coloured bar or highlight behind a key word draws the eye. Use brand colours and highlight one phrase at a time.

Session 2.2

Captions

Generating and styling captions people actually read.

Video referenceHow To Make VIRAL Captions in CapCut (Step by Step)Patrik Key · 11:25 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Auto captions create the text from speech; always proofread them.
  • Show a few words at a time and highlight the key word.
  • Place captions in the middle-lower area, clear of the platform's own text.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:15
    Font and colour

    Choose a bold, clean font that stays readable on a small screen. Use white or a light colour for most words and one brand colour for emphasis.

  2. 1:07
    Setting up

    Set the project to 9:16 and place the speaker so there is room for captions in the lower-middle of the frame.

  3. 1:54
    Generating captions

    Use auto captions to create the text from speech, then correct spelling, names and numbers line by line.

  4. 3:04
    Text layers

    Break captions into short groups of two to four words so each appears with the speech. Long lines are not read.

  5. 5:00
    Animating

    Apply one subtle entrance animation to every caption and emphasise key words with size or colour. Consistency matters more than variety.

  6. 9:17
    Gradient and glow

    A light gradient or glow can lift captions off a busy background. If it reduces readability, remove it.

Session 2.3

Audio: voice, music and sound effects

Balancing the three layers of sound.

Video referenceCapCut Audio Editing For Beginners 2025 | Everything You Need To KNOW!Vince Opra · 14:54 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Voice comes first: music should sit clearly underneath it.
  • Fade music in and out, and cut on the beat where it suits the video.
  • Only use music you are licensed to use — commercial accounts cannot use every trending sound.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:00
    Why audio matters

    Viewers forgive imperfect pictures much sooner than bad sound. Clean voice is the first priority of any edit.

  2. 0:46
    Syncing external audio

    If you recorded sound separately, line it up with the camera audio using a clap or the waveform peaks, then mute the camera track.

  3. 2:01
    Editing to the beat

    Mark the beats of the music and place cuts on them for montage sections. Not every cut needs to land on a beat.

  4. 5:39
    Choosing music

    Match the energy of the music to the video and choose tracks without vocals under speech. Use only music you are licensed to use.

  5. 7:31
    Ducking and fades

    Lower the music whenever someone speaks and raise it in gaps. Fade music in and out so it never starts or stops abruptly.

  6. 11:31
    Noise removal and J and L cuts

    Reduce background noise gently — too much sounds metallic. Let audio start before or continue after its picture for smoother, more natural cuts.

Session 3.1

Transitions

Making changes between shots feel smooth and intentional.

Video referenceYou Won’t Use CapCut Transitions Again, After This Video!Davinci · 10:43 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Most cuts need no transition at all; a clean cut is the professional default.
  • A transition works when it matches movement or meaning between the two shots.
  • Use one transition style per video so the edit feels consistent.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:00
    Why custom transitions

    Preset transitions are recognisable and overused. A transition you build yourself is designed for the two shots it joins.

  2. 0:29
    Motivation

    The best transitions are hidden in movement: something passes the lens, the camera whips, a hand covers the frame. Plan them when filming.

  3. 3:53
    Building the transition

    Overlap the two clips, then animate position, scale or a mask with keyframes so the first shot leaves as the second arrives.

  4. Part 4
    Matching movement

    Keep the direction and speed of movement the same across the cut. A move to the right should continue to the right in the next shot.

  5. Part 5
    Motion blur and sound

    A little motion blur hides the join, and a whoosh sound on the cut sells the movement.

  6. 10:09
    Working faster

    Save the finished transition as a preset or compound clip so you can reuse it — and use it sparingly.

Screenshots are frames from the video, served by YouTube. Tap a time to open the video at that part.

Practice

Build one smooth custom transition between two clips, then decide honestly whether a plain cut is better.

Session 3.2

Keyframes

Animating anything over time.

Video referenceCapCut Keyframe Mastery: Level Up Your Edits!Collin Michael · 19:47 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A keyframe records a value — position, scale, opacity — at a moment; CapCut animates between two keyframes.
  • Slow zooms on a talking head add energy without distracting.
  • Use easing or curves so movement starts and stops smoothly.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:15
    What keyframes are

    A keyframe stores the value of a setting at one point in time. Two keyframes with different values create movement between them.

  2. 0:40
    Zoom-in effect

    Set a scale keyframe at the start and a slightly larger one later for a slow push-in. Small changes — a few percent — look professional.

  3. 3:52
    Colour changes

    Colour settings can be keyframed too, for example fading from black and white to colour at a key moment.

  4. 5:28
    Volume

    Keyframe volume to lower music under speech and raise it afterwards, with smooth ramps instead of sudden jumps.

  5. 10:41
    Overlays and tilts

    Animate overlays, logos and images across the frame, and add slight rotation for energy. Keep every move purposeful.

  6. 17:56
    Tracking with keyframes

    Follow a moving subject by adjusting position every few frames so text or a graphic stays attached to it.

Screenshots are frames from the video, served by YouTube. Tap a time to open the video at that part.

Practice

Add a slow zoom-in to a talking clip and animate a logo moving into the corner.

Session 3.3

Colour grading

Correcting and styling the colour of your footage.

Video referenceFAST, EASY & FREE! Cinematic Color Grading in CapCut | Video Editing TutorialJoris Hermans · 10:00 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Correct first: exposure, white balance and contrast. Style second.
  • Keep skin tones natural — they are what viewers notice first.
  • Apply the grade with an adjustment layer so every clip matches.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:26
    Correction first

    Start by making the image look normal: set exposure and contrast, especially if the footage is flat or log.

  2. 2:57
    Saturation

    Add colour gradually. Skin should look healthy, not orange. Compare with a reference image you like.

  3. 3:06
    Temperature and tint

    Fix white balance so whites look white. Warmer feels friendly, cooler feels clean or dramatic — choose on purpose.

  4. 4:54
    Filters

    A filter is a starting look, not a finish. Reduce its strength until it supports the image without taking over.

  5. 6:30
    HSL

    Adjust single colours — for example calming a distracting green or enriching a brand colour — without affecting the rest.

  6. 7:35
    Curves

    Curves give precise control of contrast. A gentle S-shape adds depth; lift the bottom slightly for a softer, filmic black.

Correct three clips shot in different light so they match, then add one subtle look to all of them.

Session 4.1

Removing backgrounds

Cutting a subject out of its background.

Video referenceHow to Remove Video Background in CapCut (Step-by-Step Guide)Kevin Stratvert · 5:14 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Auto removal works best with good light and clear separation between subject and background.
  • Chroma key removes a single colour such as a green screen and gives cleaner edges.
  • Check hair, hands and fast movement at full size before you accept a cut-out.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:12
    On mobile

    Select the clip and use the remove-background tool. Preview the result on the whole clip, not just the first frame.

  2. 2:44
    On desktop

    The same tool is in the inspector under the video settings, with extra options for refining the edge.

  3. Part 3
    Auto removal versus chroma key

    Auto removal recognises a person without a green screen. Chroma key removes one chosen colour and gives cleaner edges when you can film on a green or blue background.

  4. Part 4
    Filming for a clean cut-out

    Light the subject evenly, keep distance from the background and avoid clothing close to the background colour.

  5. Part 5
    Adding the new background

    Place the background on the track below. Match brightness and colour so the subject belongs in the scene.

  6. Part 6
    Checking the edges

    Look at hair, fingers and fast movement. Add a slight feather or stroke only if it hides a flaw.

Session 4.2

Speed ramps

Changing speed within a clip for emphasis.

Video referenceViral Speed Ramp - Full CapCut TutorialMatt Loui · 17:28 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A speed ramp moves smoothly between fast and slow instead of jumping.
  • Slow motion needs footage shot at a high frame rate — 60 frames per second or more.
  • Place the slow part on the moment that matters and sync it to the music.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:00
    Footage for speed ramps

    Movement makes a ramp work: a walk, a car, a turn. Film at 60 frames per second or higher so slow sections stay smooth.

  2. 1:01
    Music first

    Choose the track before editing and mark its strong beats. The slow-down and speed-up should land on them.

  3. 1:47
    Stabilising

    Stabilise shaky clips before changing speed. Speed changes exaggerate camera shake.

  4. 2:36
    Basic speed ramp

    Use the speed curve: fast into the moment, slow on the moment, fast out. Drag the points until the change feels smooth.

  5. 5:01
    Matching motion

    Cut between clips while both are moving fast in the same direction, so the speed hides the cut.

  6. 10:38
    Motion blur and sound

    Add motion blur to fast sections and a whoosh or bass hit on each ramp. Sound makes the speed change feel deliberate.

Screenshots are frames from the video, served by YouTube. Tap a time to open the video at that part.

Practice

Shoot a simple action at 60 fps and build a speed ramp that slows on the key moment.

Session 4.3

Masking

Showing or hiding parts of a clip.

Video referenceHow To Use Masking in CapCut for Cool Effects! (Video Editing Tutorial)Collin Michael · 5:53 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A mask controls which area of a clip is visible.
  • Feather the edge so the mask blends instead of showing a hard line.
  • Combine masks with keyframes to reveal text or make a subject pass in front of a title.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:21
    What masking is

    A mask shows part of a clip and hides the rest. Shapes include line, circle, rectangle and custom.

  2. Part 2
    Position, size and feather

    Move and resize the mask in the player, and raise the feather so the edge blends softly.

  3. Part 3
    Inverting

    Invert swaps the visible and hidden areas — useful for cutting a hole through one clip to show another.

  4. 1:14
    Text behind a subject

    Duplicate the clip, place text between the two copies and mask the top copy to the subject so the text appears to sit behind them.

  5. Part 5
    Animating a mask

    Keyframe the mask's position or size to reveal a title, wipe between shots or follow a moving subject.

  6. Part 6
    Keeping it clean

    Zoom in to check the edge on every part of the clip, and readjust where the subject moves outside the mask.

Create a title that appears from behind a person or object using a mask.

Which AI features genuinely save time.

Session 6.1

Editing for retention

Why viewers stay or swipe, and what the editor controls.

Video referenceHow to get retention SO HIGH your shorts always go viralLOGAN SMITH · 11:16 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • The first seconds decide everything: start with the strongest moment.
  • Remove every second that does not add information, emotion or curiosity.
  • Change something regularly — shot, text, sound or zoom — to reset attention.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:30
    What retention is

    Retention is the share of viewers still watching at each second. Platforms promote videos that keep people watching.

  2. 1:19
    Composing the video

    Retention starts before the edit: one clear idea, a strong first line and a reason to stay until the end.

  3. Part 3
    The first three seconds

    Open on the most interesting moment or statement. Remove greetings, logos and long introductions.

  4. 6:11
    Editing for attention

    Cut every dead moment, change the visual regularly and use text to underline the key point. Each second must earn its place.

  5. Part 5
    The ending

    End as soon as the point is made. A clean ending that loops into the start encourages replays.

  6. 9:01
    Learning from the data

    Open the retention graph after posting. Find where viewers leave and fix that pattern in the next video.

Editing viral-style reels

The pacing, captions and sound design of high-performing short videos.

Session notes
  • Pace comes from cutting on speech and action, not from adding effects.
  • Sound effects under text and transitions make an edit feel finished.
  • Study videos that perform in your niche and note what they do in each second.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:58
    Easing

    Easing makes movement speed up and slow down naturally. Apply it to zooms, text and position changes so nothing moves mechanically.

  2. 2:26
    Sound

    Layer subtle sound effects under text, cuts and movements. Keep them quiet — they should be felt more than heard.

  3. 3:32
    Flashes and zooms

    A quick zoom or a brief flash on a beat adds energy. Use them on key moments only.

  4. 4:57
    Overlay shadows

    A soft shadow behind overlaid images and text separates them from the background and adds depth.

  5. 6:03
    Text tracking

    Attach text to a moving object or person so it follows the action. It takes time, so reserve it for hero moments.

  6. Part 6
    Restraint

    Effects support the idea. If a viewer notices the editing more than the message, remove something.

Session 6.3

Getting clients as an editor

Turning your editing into paid work.

Video referenceHow To ACTUALLY Get Clients For Video Editing (On-Demand)Sam P Dunn · 7:46 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A small portfolio of strong edits in one niche beats a large mixed one.
  • Send personal outreach that shows the client's own content improved.
  • Agree scope, revisions, price and deadlines in writing before you start.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. Part 1
    Choose a niche

    Pick one type of client — fitness coaches, restaurants, real-estate agents. You learn their needs and your examples become directly relevant.

  2. Part 2
    Build a small portfolio

    Three strong edits in that niche are enough. If you have no clients yet, re-edit public content as a demonstration and say clearly that it is a sample.

  3. Part 3
    Find the right people

    Look for businesses already posting video inconsistently or with weak editing. They understand the value and have footage.

  4. Part 4
    Personal outreach

    Send a short message that shows you looked at their content, with one specific improvement or a short sample edit. Avoid copy-and-paste messages.

  5. Part 5
    The offer

    Offer a defined package: number of videos per month, turnaround time, number of revisions and price. Clear offers are easier to accept.

  6. Part 6
    Keep the client

    Deliver on time, communicate early about problems and report results. Retained clients and referrals are worth more than constant searching.

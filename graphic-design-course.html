How this course works

  1. Start at your level. Beginner, Intermediate and Professional each have their own curriculum. New to the subject? Begin at level one.
  2. Watch, then read the notes. Every session has one trusted YouTube tutorial and short session notes with the points to remember.
  3. Do the practice task. Skills come from doing. Every session ends with something to make.
  4. Earn a certificate per level. Mark sessions complete — progress is saved in this browser — and unlock a certificate at each level.

What you need

  • Beginner level: a free Canva account is enough.
  • Intermediate and pro levels: Adobe Illustrator (free trial available) or another vector design app.
  • A sketchbook: ideas start on paper.
  • Time: about 5 hours of video across all three levels, plus practice.

Session 1.1

The principles of design

The principles designers use to organise any layout.

Video referenceThe Principles of Design | FREE COURSEEnvato · 21:47 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Contrast, alignment, repetition and proximity fix most weak designs.
  • Hierarchy tells the viewer what to read first, second and third.
  • White space is a design element, not empty space.
Practice

Find three adverts and mark where each principle is used.

Session 1.2

Typography

How to choose and set type so it is easy to read and looks intentional.

Video referenceThe ULTIMATE Guide To Typography For BeginnersDesignSpo · 13:30 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Use no more than two typefaces in one design.
  • Create hierarchy with size, weight and spacing.
  • Adjust line spacing and never stretch a font.
Practice

Set the same paragraph and headline in three type pairings and choose the clearest.

Session 1.3

Colour theory

How colours relate and how to build a palette that works.

Video reference🔸 The ONLY Colour Theory Video You Ever Need To Watch!Satori Graphics · 31:46 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Use the 60-30-10 rule: main colour, secondary colour, accent.
  • Check contrast so text is readable on its background.
  • RGB is for screens; CMYK is for print.
Practice

Build a five-colour palette for a brand and test it on a simple layout.

Session 1.4

Layout and grids

How to use grids and composition to arrange content.

Video referenceThe FULL 2026 Guide To Layout & Composition For Designers!Satori Graphics · 30:56 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A grid gives every element a reason for its position.
  • Align everything to something.
  • Group related items and separate unrelated ones.
Practice

Redesign a cluttered flyer using a simple column grid.

Session 1.5

Canva essentials

How to produce clean designs quickly in Canva.

Video referenceMASTER CANVA IN 15 MINUTES! Canva Tutorial For BeginnersAurelius Tjin · 16:22 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Start from the correct size for the platform.
  • Set up brand colours and fonts once and reuse them.
  • Edit templates heavily so the result looks like you, not the template.
Practice

Design three social posts in Canva that clearly belong to the same brand.

Session 2.1

Adobe Illustrator basics

The essential Illustrator tools for building vector graphics.

Video referenceAdobe Illustrator for Beginners: Get Started in 10 MinutesAndy Tells Things · 9:43 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Vectors are made from points and paths, so they scale without losing quality.
  • Learn the Pen tool, Shape Builder and Pathfinder first.
  • Work in artboards and name your layers.
Practice

Recreate three simple icons using basic shapes and the Shape Builder.

Go deeper Learn Adobe Illustrator 2025 in 1 Hour – Beginner’s Full Crash Course — Will Paterson · 1:09:35 (optional)

Session 2.2

The logo design process

The full process from brief to finished logo.

Video referenceThe Logo Design Process From Start To FinishMohamed Achraf · 21:12 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Research and sketch on paper before opening the computer.
  • A good logo is simple, memorable and works in one colour.
  • Test it very small and very large.
Practice

Design a logo for an invented café: 20 sketches, three refined ideas, one final.

Session 2.3

Brand and identity

What a brand is and how identity design expresses it.

Video referenceBeginning Graphic Design: Branding & IdentityLearnFree · 6:25 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A brand is what people think and feel about a business.
  • Identity is the visible system: logo, colour, type, imagery and tone.
  • Consistency builds recognition.
Practice

Collect the logo, colours, fonts and imagery of a brand you admire on one page and describe its personality.

Session 2.4

Social media post design

A repeatable process for designing strong social media graphics.

Video referenceDesign Better Social Media Posts (Full Process)GraphiqVibe · 13:14 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • One message per post.
  • Design for the feed size first and keep text large.
  • Build templates so a series looks consistent.
Practice

Design a set of five posts from one template system.

Session 2.5

Poster design

What makes a poster work from a distance and up close.

Video referenceWhat Makes A Good Poster Design? | Design LessonGareth David Studio · 10:47 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A poster needs one dominant element.
  • Hierarchy: headline, key details, then small print.
  • Leave generous margins.
Practice

Design an A3 event poster with a clear focal point and three levels of information.

Session 3.1

Brand strategy

The strategic foundations that come before any visual design.

Video referenceLearn Brand Strategy In 17 Minutes (101 Crash Course)Brand Master Academy · 17:01 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Strategy defines who the brand is for, what it stands for and why it is different.
  • Positioning is the place you hold in the customer's mind.
  • Design decisions should be traceable to the strategy.
Practice

Write a one-page brand strategy: audience, purpose, positioning, personality and tone.

Session 3.2

Designing a brand identity

How to build a full identity system around a logo.

Video referenceHow To ACTUALLY Design a Brand Identity (Full Guide 2026)Jack Watson · 31:05 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Design the system — logo variations, colour, type, patterns — not just a logo.
  • Show the identity on real applications.
  • Flexibility matters: it must work in every size and format.
Practice

Expand your café logo into an identity: logo variations, palette, type and three mock-ups.

Session 3.3

Brand guidelines

How to document an identity so that anyone can apply it correctly.

Video referenceBrand Guideline Fundamentals in 11 minutesJack Watson · 11:34 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Guidelines cover logo use, clear space, colours, type and imagery.
  • Show what not to do as well as what to do.
  • Keep it short enough that people will actually read it.
Practice

Create a six-page brand guideline for your café identity.

Session 3.4

Print-ready files

How to prepare artwork so it prints correctly the first time.

Video referenceExport a Print-Ready PDF from Adobe Illustrator (Updated for 2025)Print Design Academy - Graphic Design for Print · 19:52 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Use CMYK, 300 ppi images and 3 mm bleed unless the printer says otherwise.
  • Keep important content inside the safe margin.
  • Outline or embed fonts and export a PDF.
Practice

Prepare a business card as a print-ready PDF with bleed and crop marks.

Session 3.5

Presenting to clients

How to present design work so clients understand and approve it.

Video referenceHow To Present Logo Designs and Identity Projects to ClientsThe Futur · 24:10 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Restate the goals before showing any design.
  • Present fewer, stronger options and explain your reasoning.
  • Show designs in context, not floating on white.
Practice

Build a ten-slide presentation of your café identity.

Session 3.6

A design portfolio

How to build a portfolio that gets you hired.

Video reference🔸 The ONLY Graphic Design Portfolio Video You Need To Watch!Satori Graphics · 18:31 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Show four to six strong projects as case studies.
  • Explain the problem, your process and the result.
  • Lead with your best project.
Practice

Write up your café identity as a case study with process images.

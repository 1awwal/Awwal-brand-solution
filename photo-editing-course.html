How this course works

  1. Start at your level. Beginner, Intermediate and Professional each have their own curriculum. New to the subject? Begin at level one.
  2. Watch, then read the notes. Every session has one trusted YouTube tutorial, session notes with the points to remember, and a part-by-part guide that opens the video at each timestamp.
  3. Do the practice task. Skills come from doing. Every session ends with something to make.
  4. Earn a certificate per level. Mark sessions complete — progress is saved in this browser — and unlock a certificate at each level.

What you need

  • Software: Adobe Lightroom Classic and Photoshop (the Photography plan includes both). Free trials are available.
  • Photos: your own pictures, ideally RAW files. A phone that shoots RAW is enough.
  • Computer: any recent Mac or Windows PC with a reasonably accurate screen.
  • Time: about 5 hours of video across all three levels, plus practice.

Session 1.3

Reading the histogram

How to read the histogram so you can judge exposure by measurement, not guesswork.

Video referenceLightroom: Understanding the HistogramChristian Möhrle - The Phlog Photography · 8:27 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Left is shadows, right is highlights, height is how much of each tone.
  • A graph pushed against either edge means lost detail (clipping).
  • There is no perfect shape — a night photo should sit to the left.

How this course works

  1. Start at your level. Beginner, Intermediate and Professional each have their own curriculum. New to the subject? Begin at level one.
  2. Watch, then read the notes. Every session has one trusted YouTube tutorial and short session notes with the points to remember.
  3. Do the practice task. Skills come from doing. Every session ends with something to make.
  4. Earn a certificate per level. Mark sessions complete — progress is saved in this browser — and unlock a certificate at each level.

What you need

  • Software: Adobe Lightroom Classic and Photoshop (the Photography plan includes both). Free trials are available.
  • Photos: your own pictures, ideally RAW files. A phone that shoots RAW is enough.
  • Computer: any recent Mac or Windows PC with a reasonably accurate screen.
  • Time: about 5 hours of video across all three levels, plus practice.

LEVEL 01 · BEGINNER

Lightroom and Photoshop foundations

Start here if you have never edited a photo properly. You will learn both apps, read a histogram and make clean, natural corrections.

Session 1.1

Lightroom Classic from zero

How to import, organise, edit and export photos in Lightroom Classic.

Video reference2026 Lightroom Classic Tutorial | Full Beginner WalkthroughZac Watson · 46:06 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Lightroom never changes your original file — every edit can be undone.
  • Work top to bottom in the Develop panel: light, colour, detail.
  • Use flags and star ratings to pick your best shots before editing.
Practice

Import 30 photos, flag your best ten, and give each one a basic edit.

Session 1.2

Light: highlights, shadows, whites and blacks

What each tone slider really does and how to recover detail without making a photo look fake.

Video referenceUnderstanding Shadows and Highlights in Lightroom | Lightroom Tutorials #7PiXimperfect · 15:28 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Exposure moves everything; highlights and shadows target bright and dark areas.
  • Whites and blacks set the brightest and darkest points.
  • If a photo looks grey and flat, you have probably lifted the shadows too far.
Practice

Take one photo that is too dark and one that is too bright, and fix both using only the tone sliders.

Session 1.3

Reading the histogram

How to read the histogram so you can judge exposure by measurement, not guesswork.

Video referenceLightroom: Understanding the HistogramChristian Möhrle - The Phlog Photography · 8:27 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Left is shadows, right is highlights, height is how much of each tone.
  • A graph pushed against either edge means lost detail (clipping).
  • There is no perfect shape — a night photo should sit to the left.
Practice

Edit three photos while watching the histogram and stop each one just before it clips.

Session 1.4

Photoshop from zero

The Photoshop workspace, the essential tools and how a document is built.

Video referencePhotoshop for Beginners: 30-Minute Masterclass!PiXimperfect · 26:01 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Photoshop edits pixels; Lightroom edits whole photos quickly.
  • Learn the shortcuts V (move), B (brush), Ctrl/Cmd+Z (undo) and Ctrl/Cmd+T (transform).
  • Save working files as PSD and export copies as JPEG or PNG.
Practice

Open a photo, crop it, straighten it, and export it as a JPEG.

Go deeper Photoshop for Complete Beginners | Lesson 1 — PiXimperfect · 45:34 (optional)

Session 1.5

Layers and layer masks

How layers stack and how masks hide or reveal parts of a layer without deleting anything.

Video referenceLayer Mask - Photoshop for Beginners | Lesson 4PiXimperfect · 29:26 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Layers work like sheets of glass stacked on top of each other.
  • On a mask, white reveals and black conceals.
  • Masks are non-destructive: paint with white to bring anything back.
Practice

Place two photos in one document and blend them with a layer mask and a soft brush.

Colour, selections and retouching

Session 2.1

Lightroom masking

How to adjust only the sky, the subject or any area you choose.

Video referenceUse Lightroom Masks Like A Pro! The Secret To Better PhotosTKNORTH · 15:32 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Select Subject and Select Sky create masks in one click.
  • Combine masks with add, subtract and intersect for precise control.
  • Brighten the subject slightly and darken the edges to guide the eye.
Practice

Edit a portrait and a landscape using at least three masks in each.

Session 2.2

Colour grading in Lightroom

How to shape colour with white balance, the colour mixer (HSL) and colour grading wheels.

Video referenceThe Trick to Perfect Color Grades in LightroomSean Dalton · 21:21 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Hue changes the colour, saturation its strength, luminance its brightness.
  • Fewer colours in a photo usually look more professional.
  • Warm highlights with cool shadows is a classic, reliable grade.
Practice

Create two different colour grades of the same photo and save your favourite as a preset.

Session 2.3

Selections and background removal

How to cut a subject out cleanly, including hair and soft edges.

Video referenceRemove Backgrounds In Photoshop 2025 [EASY For Beginners!]Photoshop Training Channel · 14:17 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Start with Select Subject, then refine with Select and Mask.
  • Output the selection to a layer mask, never delete the background.
  • Check the cut-out against both a light and a dark background.
Practice

Cut out a person and a product, and place each on a plain coloured background.

Session 2.4

Natural skin retouching

A professional approach to cleaning skin while keeping real texture.

Video referenceHigh-End Skin Retouching for Beginners | Photoshop Frequency Separation TutorialEkow Dediha Photography · 13:03 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Remove temporary blemishes; keep permanent features unless asked.
  • Frequency separation splits colour and tone from texture so you can fix each alone.
  • Zoom out often — if the retouch is visible, it is too much.
Practice

Retouch a close-up portrait and compare before and after at 100% and full view.

Session 2.5

Removing unwanted objects

How to remove distractions using the Remove tool, cloning, healing and generative fill.

Video referenceRemove Objects Seamlessly In Photoshop [Clean Results]Photoshop Training Channel · 9:53 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Work on a new empty layer so the original stays untouched.
  • The Remove tool is fast; the Clone Stamp gives you exact control.
  • Check for repeating patterns that give the edit away.
Practice

Remove three distractions from a photo — for example a sign, a cable and a person in the background.

Session 3.1

Non-destructive editing with smart objects

How smart objects and adjustment layers keep every decision editable.

Video referenceEdit Non-Destructively with Smart Objects in Photoshop | Day 13PHLEARN · 13:38 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A smart object keeps the original quality even after resizing.
  • Filters applied to smart objects stay editable as smart filters.
  • Use adjustment layers instead of editing pixels directly.
Practice

Rebuild an earlier edit using only smart objects and adjustment layers.

Session 3.2

Realistic compositing

How to place a subject into a new background so that it looks real.

Video referenceHow To Match a Subject Into ANY Background In Photoshop! Compositing TutorialPhotoshop Training Channel · 30:15 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Match the light direction first — nothing else can fix a mismatch.
  • Then match brightness, contrast, colour and sharpness.
  • Add a contact shadow where the subject touches the ground.
Practice

Composite a person or product into a new scene and add matching shadows.

Session 3.3

Product photo retouching

How to clean, shape and polish product photos for e-commerce and advertising.

Video referenceHow To Create High-End Product Photos With Photoshop!Brendan Williams · 15:23 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Remove dust and scratches first, at 100% zoom.
  • Even out reflections and gradients to show the product's shape.
  • Keep colours accurate — customers will compare the photo with the real item.
Practice

Retouch a product photo on a clean background, ready for an online store.

Session 3.4

Batch editing and presets

How to edit hundreds of photos consistently using sync, presets and copy-paste settings.

Video reference3 Ways to Batch Edit Your Photos in Lightroom and Lightroom ClassicTerry Lee White · 19:41 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Edit one photo well, then sync its settings to similar shots.
  • Presets are a starting point: always adjust exposure and white balance per photo.
  • Group photos by lighting situation before syncing.
Practice

Edit a set of at least 40 photos from one shoot in under an hour.

Session 3.5

Export for web, social and print

The right export settings for each destination so photos stay sharp and accurate.

Video referenceThe Best Export Settings for Lightroom (Instagram, Facebook, Web, Print)Austin James Jackson · 13:38 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Use sRGB for anything viewed on screens.
  • For web and social, export around 2000–2500 px on the long edge as JPEG.
  • For print, export at full size and 300 ppi and ask the lab for their preferred format.
Practice

Export one photo three ways: Instagram, website and print.

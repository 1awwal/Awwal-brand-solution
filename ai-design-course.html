Session 1.1

What generative AI is

Where generative AI sits inside the wider field of AI, and what it can and cannot do.

Video referenceAI, Machine Learning, Deep Learning and Generative AI ExplainedIBM Technology · 10:01 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • AI is the broad field; machine learning learns from data; deep learning uses neural networks; generative AI creates new content.
  • A model has no understanding of your business — it only knows patterns from its training and what you tell it.
  • Treat AI as a fast junior assistant: useful for drafts and options, but you stay responsible for the result.
Detailed notes · 5 parts
  1. Part 1
    Artificial intelligence

    AI is the broad goal of making machines perform tasks that normally need human intelligence: recognising, deciding, translating, creating. Everything else in this session is a smaller circle inside this one.

  2. Part 2
    Machine learning

    Instead of being programmed with rules, a machine-learning system is shown many examples and finds the patterns itself. The quality of its answers depends on the quality and range of the examples it learned from.

  3. Part 3
    Deep learning

    Deep learning uses neural networks with many layers. It is what made modern image recognition, speech and language tools possible, and it needs very large amounts of data and computing power.

  4. Part 4
    Generative AI and foundation models

    Generative models produce new text, images, audio or video rather than only classifying what already exists. Large 'foundation' models are trained once on huge data sets, then used for many tasks through prompts.

  5. Part 5
    What this means for a designer

    The model does not know your client, your brand or today's facts unless you supply them. Use it to produce options and drafts quickly, then apply your own judgement, taste and checking.

Session 1.2

How language models work

Why a chat assistant behaves the way it does, so you can prompt it with realistic expectations.

Video referenceLarge Language Models explained briefly3Blue1Brown · 7:58 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A language model predicts the next word again and again; that is why answers can sound confident and still be wrong.
  • The model only sees what is in the conversation. Missing context produces generic answers.
  • Always check facts, names, prices and figures yourself before they reach a client.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:00
    How chatbots work

    A chat assistant is a program that continues a piece of text. Your message and the conversation so far are the text; the reply is its continuation, produced one small piece at a time.

  2. 0:35
    Large language models

    The model assigns a probability to every possible next word and picks from the likely ones. Because there is some randomness, the same prompt can give different answers — useful for options, risky for facts.

  3. 1:28
    Training and parameters

    The model's behaviour is stored in billions of numbers called parameters. Training adjusts them using enormous amounts of text until its predictions improve. Nobody writes the rules by hand.

  4. 3:46
    Pre-training and human feedback

    First the model learns general language from raw text. Then it is refined with human feedback so it behaves like a helpful assistant. This is why it has a tone and limits, not only knowledge.

  5. 4:42
    Transformers and attention

    The transformer design lets every word 'pay attention' to the other words around it, so meaning depends on context. This is why giving clear context in your prompt changes the answer so much.

  6. 6:27
    Emergent behaviour

    The model's skills emerge from training rather than being designed line by line, so it can surprise even its makers — in good and bad ways. Fluent and confident does not mean correct.

Session 1.3

Prompt engineering basics

A simple structure for prompts that gets useful answers the first time.

Video referenceGoogle's 9 Hour AI Prompt Engineering Course In 20 MinutesTina Huang · 20:17 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A strong prompt states the task, the context, any references and the format you want back.
  • Be specific about audience, tone, length and what to avoid.
  • Prompting is a conversation: evaluate the answer, then refine instead of starting again.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:57
    The fundamentals: a prompt framework

    Build each prompt from the same parts: the task, the context, references or examples, how you will evaluate the answer, and how you will iterate. Adding a persona and an output format makes the task sharper.

  2. 3:45
    Four ways to iterate

    When the answer is not right: revisit the framework and add what is missing, break the task into shorter sentences, try a similar task phrased differently, or add constraints such as length, tone and audience.

  3. 5:51
    Multimodal prompting

    You can prompt with images, audio and files as well as words. Say exactly what the model should do with the attachment — describe it, critique it, extract text or match its style.

  4. 8:09
    Everyday examples

    Emails, summaries, plans and social posts all improve when you name the reader, the goal and the tone. Keep prompts that work in a personal library so you can reuse them.

  5. 12:15
    Advanced techniques

    Prompt chaining feeds one answer into the next step. Asking the model to reason step by step, or to explore several approaches before choosing, improves harder problems.

  6. 16:03
    Building an assistant

    Give a persona, context, rules for the conversation and a stop phrase to create a reusable assistant — for example a client-feedback simulator or a brief-writing coach.

Session 2.1

Writing image prompts

How to describe a picture so an image model can make it.

Video referenceHow to Write Great Prompts for AI Image GeneratorsLearn With Shopify · 20:38 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Describe the subject first, then setting, style, lighting, colour and composition.
  • Name the medium — photograph, 3D render, flat illustration — because it changes everything.
  • Change one thing at a time between attempts so you learn what each word does.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 2:15
    What prompt engineering means for images

    An image model turns your description into a picture. It follows concrete visual words much better than abstract ones: say what is in the frame, not how you want to feel.

  2. 3:35
    A step-by-step prompt

    Write in this order: subject, action, setting, then style. Add lighting, colour and mood last. Reading the prompt aloud is a quick test — could a photographer shoot it from your words?

  3. 6:44
    Lenses and angles

    Photography language controls the picture: a wide lens shows the scene, a long lens isolates the subject, macro shows detail. Low angles add power, top-down suits food and flat lays.

  4. 8:04
    Styles

    Name the medium and the style precisely: studio product photograph, watercolour, flat vector illustration, 3D clay render. One clear style gives cleaner results than three mixed together.

  5. 11:55
    Using a chat assistant to write prompts

    Describe your idea in plain language and ask an assistant to turn it into an image prompt. Then edit it yourself — remove anything you did not intend.

  6. 14:40
    Negative prompts and seeds

    A negative prompt lists what to keep out, such as text or extra objects. A seed number lets a tool repeat the same starting point, so you can change one word and compare fairly.

Session 2.2

Canva's AI tools

The AI features inside Canva and where they save real time.

Video referenceNEW CANVA AI TOOLS ✨ (how to use CANVA MAGIC STUDIO)Kate Hayes · 13:52 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Use AI generation for first drafts and layout ideas, then adjust type, spacing and brand colours yourself.
  • Background removal, expand and erase tools fix photos faster than rebuilding them.
  • Resize one finished design into every format instead of redesigning each one.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:40
    Magic Studio overview

    Canva groups its AI features in one place. Think of them as three kinds of help: generating something new, editing what you have, and adapting a finished design to other formats.

  2. 4:45
    Styling text and shapes

    AI effects can restyle text and shapes from a description. Use them sparingly for a headline or sticker — body text should stay plain and readable.

  3. 6:52
    Expanding an image

    Expand extends a photo beyond its edges to fit a new shape, such as turning a square photo into a wide banner. Check the new areas for repeated patterns or odd details.

  4. 8:18
    Resizing and converting a design

    One finished design can be converted into other sizes and document types. Always check each version by hand: line breaks, logo size and safe margins rarely survive untouched.

  5. 9:56
    Generating images and video

    Text-to-image inside the editor is useful for backgrounds and concept images. Describe subject, style and colours, and choose the result that matches the brand rather than the most dramatic one.

  6. 11:26
    Generating a full design

    Starting from a prompt or an uploaded photo gives layout ideas quickly. Treat them as first drafts: replace the fonts and colours with the brand kit and rewrite the copy.

Session 2.3

Adobe Firefly and Generative Fill

Editing real photographs with AI inside Photoshop.

Video referencePhotoshop Ai Generative Fill Tutorial! - Complete GuideTao Prompts · 12:49 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Select the area first, then describe only what should appear inside the selection.
  • Generative Expand extends a photo to fit a new format such as a wide banner.
  • Each result is a separate layer — compare the variations and mask the best parts together.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:12
    Generative Expand

    Drag the crop beyond the edge of the photo and let Photoshop fill the new area. Leave the prompt empty to continue the existing scene, or describe what should appear.

  2. 2:04
    Creating mockups

    Select a surface such as a blank wall, screen or packaging and generate a scene around your design. It is a fast way to present a concept before a real shoot or render.

  3. 3:39
    Object removal

    Make a loose selection around the unwanted object, including its shadow, and generate with an empty prompt. Zoom in afterwards and repair any smeared texture.

  4. 5:43
    Generating backgrounds

    Select the subject, invert the selection and describe the new background. Match the direction and colour of light, or the subject will look pasted on.

  5. 7:13
    Inpainting and faces

    Generate small areas at a time for better detail. Be careful with faces and hands — regenerate or retouch by hand, and never alter a real person's appearance without consent.

  6. 9:30
    Reference images and finishing

    A reference image steers the look of the generated area. Finish with normal retouching tools; every generation sits on its own layer, so you can mask and blend the best parts.

Session 3.1

Zero-shot, few-shot and chain of thought

Three core prompting techniques and when each one helps.

Video referenceMaster AI Prompting: Zero-Shot, Few-Shot & Chain of Thought ExplainedProf. Ryan Ahmed · 10:58 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Zero-shot asks with instructions only; few-shot adds examples of the output you want.
  • Examples are the fastest way to copy a tone of voice or a format.
  • For complex problems, ask the model to reason step by step before giving its final answer.
Detailed notes · 5 parts
  1. 0:00
    What prompting is

    A prompt is the full set of instructions and material you give the model. Small changes in wording can change the result, so treat prompts as something you design and test.

  2. 2:12
    Key prompt components

    The main parts are the instruction, the context, the input data and the output indicator — the form you want the answer in. Missing parts are the usual cause of weak answers.

  3. 5:23
    Zero-shot prompting

    You give instructions with no examples. It works well for common tasks, and it is where you should start. If the format or tone is wrong, move to few-shot.

  4. 6:33
    Few-shot prompting

    You include a few examples of input and ideal output. The model copies their pattern, length and voice. Use varied examples so it learns the pattern, not one specific answer.

  5. 7:38
    Chain of thought

    Ask the model to work through the problem in steps before giving the final answer. It helps with calculation, planning and comparison — for example weighing three layout options against a brief.

Practice

Give an assistant three of your best past captions as examples and ask for five new ones. Compare with a no-example attempt.

Session 3.2

Structuring a professional prompt

How a long, reliable prompt is put together, from a team that builds the models.

Video referencePrompting 101 | Code w/ ClaudeAnthropic · 24:53 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Start with the role and the task, then give background, then detailed instructions, then the output format.
  • Separate materials from instructions with clear labels or tags so nothing gets confused.
  • Test the prompt on real cases, read the failures and fix the prompt — not just the one answer.
Detailed notes · 7 parts
  1. 1:28
    Setting the stage

    Start by telling the model what the job is and what a good result looks like. A first attempt with a thin prompt shows you what the model assumes when you say too little.

  2. 3:57
    Prompt structure

    A dependable order: the task and role, the background material, detailed instructions, examples, a reminder of the most important rules, then the output format.

  3. 5:40
    Task context

    Explain who the model is acting for and why the task matters. Context changes its choices — a caption for a luxury brand and for a student café need different decisions.

  4. 8:47
    Background detail

    Put reference material — a brief, a transcript, brand guidelines — in clearly labelled sections, separate from your instructions, so the model can refer to it precisely.

  5. 13:07
    Examples

    Examples are the strongest steering tool. Show what good output looks like and, where useful, what to avoid. Label them so the model knows they are examples, not material to copy word for word.

  6. 15:55
    Reminders

    Repeat the critical rules near the end of a long prompt: accuracy over guessing, what to do when information is missing, and anything that must never appear.

  7. 20:04
    Output formatting

    Say exactly how the answer should be laid out — headings, a table, a fixed list of fields. A predictable format is what makes a prompt reusable in a real workflow.

Write a reusable prompt that turns a client's messy brief into a one-page creative brief. Test it on two briefs.

Session 3.3

Midjourney prompts and parameters

Prompting a dedicated image model and using its settings.

Video referenceMidjourney Website Beginner's Guide: Basic Prompting, Tips & Techniques for CustomizationWoollyfern · 19:48 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Short, concrete descriptions often beat long lists of adjectives.
  • Parameters control the frame: aspect ratio, stylisation and variety are set separately from the description.
  • Use variations and upscales to explore one promising image instead of rerolling everything.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 1:58
    A tour of the website

    The create page is where you prompt, the organise page stores your images, and the explore page shows other people's work with their prompts — a good place to learn what words do.

  2. 3:39
    Your first prompt

    Begin with a short, concrete description of the subject. Each prompt returns four options; you pick the direction rather than expecting one perfect image.

  3. 4:29
    Building on a prompt

    Add one element at a time: setting, style, lighting. If a new word makes things worse, remove it. Long prompts dilute attention across too many ideas.

  4. 6:19
    Variations and upscaling

    Subtle variations keep the composition and change details; strong variations explore further. Upscale only the image you intend to use.

  5. 8:22
    Remix, pan, zoom and the editor

    Remix changes the prompt while keeping the image as a starting point. Pan and zoom extend the frame. The editor repaints a selected area — useful for fixing one wrong detail.

  6. 12:08
    Parameters

    Parameters are settings added to the prompt: aspect ratio sets the frame shape, stylise controls how artistic the result is, and chaos increases variety. Change one parameter at a time.

Session 4.1

Style and consistency

Keeping a set of AI images looking like one family.

Video referenceMidjourney SREF Complete Guide (2025) | Style References Explained from Basic to Advanced TipsWoollyfern · 17:33 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A style reference passes the look of an image without copying its content.
  • Fix your style words, palette and reference images, then change only the subject.
  • Save the references and prompts that work — they become your style library.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. Part 1
    What a style reference is

    A style reference tells the model to borrow the look of an image — colour, texture, lighting, line quality — while your prompt still decides the subject. It is the main tool for making a set of images match.

  2. Part 2
    Choosing reference images

    Pick references with a clear, consistent style and no strong subject you do not want copied. Your own previous outputs make excellent references for a brand series.

  3. Part 3
    Style weight

    A weight setting controls how strongly the reference is applied. Low values give a hint of the style; high values can override your prompt. Find the lowest value that keeps the set consistent.

  4. Part 4
    Combining references

    Several references blend together. Keep to two or three with a shared feeling, otherwise the result becomes muddy and unpredictable.

  5. Part 5
    Style codes and libraries

    Saved style codes let you reuse a look without uploading an image. Keep a simple library: the code or image, the prompt pattern and a sample result, so the whole team can produce matching work.

  6. Part 6
    Using it responsibly

    Use your own work, licensed images or abstract styles as references. Do not use a living artist's work to imitate their style for a client.

Session 4.2

Brand identity with AI

Where AI helps in a branding project — and where it does not.

Video referenceAI Branding Workflow: How to Create a Brand Identity with AIAI Master · 10:51 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Use chat assistants for research, naming options, positioning and tone of voice.
  • Use image models for mood boards and direction, not for the final logo file.
  • Redraw the chosen mark as a clean vector yourself and check it is not too close to existing brands.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:00
    Starting from a brand idea

    Begin with the brand in words: who it is for, what it stands for and three adjectives for its personality. The clearer this is, the more consistent every generated asset will be.

  2. 0:32
    First applications and colour variations

    Test the identity on a real object early — a shirt, a cup, a sign. Generating several colour variations side by side makes choosing a palette a visual decision, not a guess.

  3. 1:35
    Refining the direction

    Choose one direction and refine it with small instructions instead of generating new ideas forever. A decision made early saves hours later.

  4. 1:52
    A reusable brand kit

    Collect the logo, colours and fonts in one kit and reuse it for every piece. Remove the logo's background so it can sit cleanly on any image.

  5. 2:50
    Poster and social post

    Apply the kit to a poster, then a matching social post. Keep the same grid, type sizes and spacing so the pieces feel related.

  6. 3:48
    Consistency and editability

    Lay everything out together and check it reads as one brand. Prefer tools where the text stays editable — fixing a date should not require regenerating the image.

Session 5.1

Advanced prompt engineering

How experienced prompt engineers think about writing, testing and improving prompts.

Video referenceAI prompt engineering: A deep diveAnthropic · 1:16:43 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Write for the model as you would brief an intelligent newcomer: full context, no assumed knowledge.
  • Read the outputs closely. Most problems come from something unclear or missing in the prompt.
  • Keep test cases and re-run them whenever you change a prompt.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 2:05
    What prompt engineering is

    It is clear communication with a model, plus the engineering habit of testing and iterating. The writing matters, but so does understanding how the prompt fits the whole system.

  2. 6:34
    What makes a good prompt engineer

    Clear writing, willingness to iterate many times, and thinking about unusual cases. Reading the model's outputs carefully is the core skill.

  3. 12:17
    Refining prompts

    Write down everything the model needs to know, as if briefing a capable person who knows nothing about your project. When it fails, ask what was ambiguous instead of adding random rules.

  4. 24:27
    Honesty, personas and metaphors

    Describe the real situation and the real task rather than relying on a pretend role. A role can help, but accurate context helps more.

  5. 37:12
    Model reasoning

    Letting the model reason before answering usually improves results, and reading that reasoning shows you where the prompt misled it.

  6. 50:52
    Tips to improve

    Read good prompts, read many outputs, show your prompts to another person, and try tasks at the edge of what the model can do. Skill comes from practice, not templates.

Session 5.2

Reusable assistants: custom GPTs and Projects

Packaging instructions and brand knowledge into an assistant your team can reuse.

Video referenceHow to Create Custom GPT | OpenAI TutorialKevin Stratvert · 20:26 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A custom assistant combines standing instructions with reference files such as brand guidelines.
  • Write instructions as rules with reasons, and include examples of good output.
  • Never upload confidential client material without permission, and check each tool's data settings.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:59
    What a custom GPT is

    A version of the assistant with its own standing instructions, knowledge files and abilities, so a recurring task starts ready instead of from a blank chat.

  2. 3:16
    Creating one by conversation

    You can describe what you want and let the builder draft it. Review the draft carefully — it is a starting point, not the final instructions.

  3. 5:08
    Writing instructions

    State the role, the audience, the steps to follow, the tone and what to do when information is missing. Give reasons for rules so the assistant applies them sensibly.

  4. 9:13
    Adding knowledge

    Upload brand guidelines, tone-of-voice documents and approved examples. Keep files short and current; old files produce confident but outdated answers.

  5. 11:04
    Few-shot examples

    Include examples of ideal output inside the instructions. For a brand-voice assistant, three approved posts teach more than a page of adjectives.

  6. 12:19
    Capabilities, actions and publishing

    Turn on only the abilities the assistant needs. Decide who can use it — only you, your team or the public — and never include confidential client material in a shared assistant.

Session 5.3

AI agents and automation

What AI agents are and how they differ from a chat.

Video referenceAI Agents, Clearly ExplainedJeff Su · 10:09 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • A chat answers; a workflow follows fixed steps; an agent decides its own steps using tools.
  • Automate work that is repetitive and easy to check, such as resizing, renaming and first drafts.
  • Keep a human review step before anything is published or sent to a client.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 0:00
    AI versus AI agents

    The words are used loosely. The useful distinction is who decides the steps: you, a fixed process, or the model itself.

  2. 1:04
    Level 1: language models

    A chat model responds to a prompt with what it knows. It is passive and cannot see your files, calendar or live information unless connected to them.

  3. 2:17
    Level 2: AI workflows

    A workflow follows a path a human designed: fetch this, summarise that, post the result. It is predictable, and it cannot adapt if the path is wrong.

  4. 5:26
    Level 3: AI agents

    An agent is given a goal. It reasons about what to do, uses tools to act, looks at the result and tries again until the goal is met.

  5. 7:48
    A real-world example

    Think of a studio task such as finding suitable stock clips for a brief: an agent can search, review and shortlist. You still approve what gets used.

  6. 9:10
    Summary

    Use a chat for thinking, a workflow for repeatable steps and an agent for open-ended tasks — always with a human check before anything is published.

Session 6.2

Upscaling and production-ready files

Getting AI images to print and screen quality.

Video referenceAfter 10yrs of Testing, This is the BEST Upscaler. Period.PiXimperfect · 16:18 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • Generated images are low resolution: upscale before print and check at 100% zoom.
  • Know the target — roughly 300 pixels per inch at final print size.
  • Retouch artefacts, replace AI text with real type and export in the right colour profile.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. 1:28
    What an AI upscaler does

    It does not simply enlarge pixels: it invents plausible detail. That is powerful, and it means the result can drift from the original.

  2. 4:07
    Photo restoration

    Old or damaged photos gain clarity, but faces can change subtly. Compare with the original and keep the person recognisable.

  3. 5:47
    Clothing, jewellery and skin

    Fine patterns, stitching and skin texture are where upscalers succeed or fail. Avoid plastic-looking skin; lower the strength if detail looks artificial.

  4. 9:02
    Product photos

    For products, accuracy matters more than drama. Check logos, labels and text closely — invented letters are common and unacceptable for a client.

  5. 10:26
    Landscapes and difficult cases

    Natural textures upscale well. Missed focus can be partly rescued, but it is a repair, not a replacement for a sharp original.

  6. 14:40
    Pros, cons and print

    Decide the final print size first, upscale to roughly 300 pixels per inch at that size, then inspect at 100% zoom before sending to print.

Session 6.3

Copyright, ethics and client work

The legal and ethical questions every AI-assisted designer must be able to answer.

Video referenceAI Art: Copyright, Ownership and Infringement (oh my!)Extra Credits · 9:14 · YouTube ↗

Session notes
  • In many countries purely AI-generated work may not be protected by copyright; human authorship matters. Laws differ and change — check the rules where you work.
  • Do not prompt for a living artist's style or a competitor's brand assets.
  • Tell clients how you use AI, read each tool's commercial terms and put it in your contract.
Detailed notes · 6 parts
  1. Part 1
    Who owns AI output

    Copyright traditionally protects work made by a human author. In several countries, material generated by AI alone may have little or no protection. Human selection, arrangement and editing can change that.

  2. Part 2
    Training data and infringement

    Models learn from huge collections of existing work, and the legal questions around that are still being decided in courts. Output that closely resembles a specific existing work can infringe regardless of how it was made.

  3. Part 3
    Style and living artists

    A style is generally not protected, but deliberately imitating a named living artist for commercial work is an ethical problem and a reputational risk. Do not do it.

  4. Part 4
    Brands, logos and likeness

    Trademarks and a person's likeness are protected separately from copyright. Never generate other brands' marks or real people for commercial use without permission.

  5. Part 5
    Tool licences

    Each tool's terms decide whether you may use the output commercially and whether your uploads are used for training. Read them, and prefer tools with clear commercial terms for client work.

  6. Part 6
    What to do in practice

    Disclose AI use to clients, keep your working files and prompts as evidence of your contribution, and take legal advice for high-value work. Laws differ by country and are changing.
